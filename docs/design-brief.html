00

The problem is a trigger, not a tab

You press enter. The agent starts. For the next forty seconds to twelve minutes you have no task, and crucially, you have a decision to make about what to do with that time. Boredom plus an open decision is the exact entry condition for the habit loop. YouTube wins because it is the cheapest answer to an unwanted question.

So the fix cannot be a better menu, a better inbox, or a nicer session list. Any of those still asks you the question. The fix has to answer the question for you, automatically, on the dispatch event itself, before the question can form.

That framing came from your earlier conversation and it is correct. Everything below takes it as the requirement and asks a narrower engineering question: what is the most reliable way to detect the gap, and what happens on the way back.

The finding that changes the design

Your hooks never fire in Cowork because Cowork does not use your home config. It creates a fresh, session scoped .claude directory per session and points the bundled CLI at that. Here is the actual directory listing from your most recent Cowork session:

$ ls -a ".../local-agent-mode-sessions/<space>/<project>/local_5781c1f0.../.claude"

.claude.json    backups     plugins             projects
CLAUDE.md       policy-limits.json  sessions    tasks

No settings.json. That file is where hooks live, so no hooks are registered.

But that same private home contains the ordinary transcript, and the transcript is exactly the event stream we want:

$ head .../.claude/projects/<slug>/f5c52995-....jsonl

0 | type= queue-operation  ts= 2026-07-30T19:35:05.414Z
1 | type= queue-operation  ts= 2026-07-30T19:35:05.531Z
2 | type= user            ts= 2026-07-30T19:35:05.551Z   keys= [cwd, entrypoint,
      gitBranch, message, origin, parentUuid, permissionMode, promptId,
      promptSource, sessionId, timestamp]
3 | type= attachment       ts= 2026-07-30T19:35:05.550Z

Read structurally, keys only. No message content was opened.

A line of type: "user" carrying a promptId and a millisecond timestamp appears the instant you submit. That is the dispatch event, in Cowork, on disk, with no cooperation from Anthropic required. It is not a heuristic and it is not a guess. It is the same event the hook would have given us, arriving a fraction of a second later.

This matters because the earlier design, having concluded Cowork was closed, fell back to inferring the gap from your behaviour: frontmost application plus keyboard idle time. That inference is the weakest part of that plan, and we do not need it.

04

How it works

One small background daemon, five stages. The stages are numbered because they really are a sequence: each one hands a fact to the next.

01

Detect

Two push sources, one queue, and no polling in either. In Claude Code, a UserPromptSubmit hook fires synchronously the moment you submit. In Cowork, where hooks are unavailable, a single recursive FSEvents watch on the sessions root has the kernel wake the process on write; a gap starts on any new type: "user" line. The gap ends on the matching assistant completion, on a Stop hook, or on a permission prompt, whichever lands first.

Measured on this machine: a recursive watch fired 13ms after an append to a file six levels deep in a directory tree that did not exist when the watch started, which is exactly what a brand new Cowork session looks like. Between events the daemon does nothing at all.

Idle time stays in the design, demoted. It never starts a gap on its own. It only vetoes one, so that a gap opened while you are clearly still typing does not route you anywhere.

02

Decide

At 25 seconds the gap is real. The router picks exactly one activity and never presents a list, because a list at this moment is the decision fatigue you asked to remove, with extra steps. It picks from a ladder you wrote in advance, filtered by live state: how many Anki cards are actually due, whether a book is open, whether you already did this rung in the last gap.

If the agent is still working later, the router escalates rather than re-deciding. A rung that is exhausted, for example a deck you have emptied, hands off to the next one down.

03

Deliver

Put the activity in front of you with no further input. Anki gets launched and driven straight into a review through AnkiConnect, landing on a card rather than a deck list. Kindle opens by URL to the book you are reading. The to do list opens the vault to one specific note. The queue rung raises a small capture window that is already focused and accepting text.

Nothing is closed, hidden or quit. Your Cowork window keeps its exact state and stays one keystroke behind.

04

Reclaim

The moment the agent finishes or needs you, the system pulls you back: Cowork comes forward, the activity drops behind, and the notification says which session wants you and what it wants. This is the stage the earlier plan leaves as a stub and it is the one that decides whether you trust the whole thing.

Trust is the actual product here. If you believe you will be fetched, you can let go. If you do not, you will check, and checking reintroduces exactly the moment we deleted.

05

Learn and show

Every gap is written down: when it opened, how long it ran, which rung fired, whether you stayed, and what you were doing when it closed. A local page renders the record. That gives the 25 second threshold a real number instead of a guess, and it answers the question your earlier conversation ended on: whether the boredom was ever the trigger, or whether the vice arrives anyway.

Never route into nothing

A rung that opens an empty deck teaches you the system is noise. Every rung checks live state first and falls through if it has no work for you.

One key moves you down the ladder

Landed on cards but want the book instead? One key advances to the next rung. It is a next, not a menu, so there is still nothing to choose between. Hold it and you walk the ladder in order.

One escape, always the same

A different single key stands the router down for the rest of that gap. Leaving is cheap on purpose. What gets deleted is the choosing, not your control.

Cooldown

After a reclaim, the router stays quiet for 90 seconds. Rapid fire prompts should not throw you between windows.

No blackout by default

Your last session ran at 2:20 in the morning. Quiet hours are available and off unless you ask for them.

07

The decisions

Five are settled and written into the loop. One is still open. Answer them in any form and I will fold them in before writing a line of code.

1. Does it take focus, or offer it?

Settled
Take it. No countdown, no confirmation. Anki simply appears in front of you. The consequence: misfires are now expensive, so the idle veto and the frontmost app guard become load bearing rather than nice to have. Both are already in Phase 1 and Phase 3 of the loop.

2. What is on the ladder, in your order?

Settled
Flashcards, then reading, then queue the next prompt, then the to do list. Plus the ability to switch between them. Switching is one key that advances to the next rung, wrapping at the end. Deliberately a next rather than a menu: a menu at that moment is the decision fatigue you asked to delete, wearing a different hat. One key changes what you are doing without ever asking you what you would like to be doing. Holding the key walks the ladder in order, so getting from cards to your book is two presses and no thought.

3. Is the to do list a real rung, or the fallback?

Settled by 2

Last on the ladder, so it fires only when everything above it is empty. That matches my read of it: writing a to do list is itself a decision heavy activity, which makes it a poor thing to be dropped into when the goal is fewer decisions. It is still always reachable by pressing the advance key.

4. Both surfaces, or Cowork first?

Settled by the data

Both. Your CLI history holds 1,883 real prompts and 139 hours of qualifying gap time, so this stopped being a judgement call once it was measured. Cowork uses the transcript watcher, Claude Code uses a hook, and both feed one queue.

5. Should distraction cost something during a gap?

Wrong question

No. Nothing is blocked, delayed, or policed. I raised this and I was wrong to. This project is the answer to distraction: drop you into something productive before the temptation has a moment to form. Bolting a blocker onto it would be solving the same problem twice, the second time with a cage. The router competes by being there first, and that is the entire mechanism.

6. The name and the home

Open

I have called it Interstice and would put it at ~/Developer/interstice, per your rule that anything that runs lives under Developer. Both are trivial to change now and annoying to change later.

08

The goal loop

The checklist is written and sitting at ~/Developer/interstice/GOAL_LOOP.md. It is fully autonomous: every item carries a check a machine can run, and none of them waits on you to look at something.

It runs in eight phases. Preflight proves the two dependencies that can silently kill everything, the Anki socket and the transcript watcher, and refuses to continue until both are demonstrated on real events. Then detection, routing, delivery, reclaim, the dashboard, and a soak phase that runs against genuine gaps and holds the loop open until the false positive rate is under one in twenty.

Because it builds a dashboard, it carries the UI to UX convergence sub loop twice: once after the design pass and once after the code pass, with a synthetic reviewer and a separate judge ruling each piece of feedback worthwhile or not. The loop cannot close while any worthwhile item is open.

Nothing in this document is running. No daemon has been installed, no file outside the checklist has been created, and no setting on your machine has been changed. The next move is yours.
