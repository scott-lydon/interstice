Strategy, tactics, and guiding principles

The distinction is older than the vocabulary that now carries it. A tactic answers the question in front of you; a strategy chooses which questions you will let yourself be asked.

What survives the translation into practice is rarely the elegant half. The principle that reads well on the page is the one that bends first under a real constraint, and the crude rule that no one wanted to write down is the one still standing at the end of the day.

So the useful question is not which principle is true, but which one is load-bearing: remove it, and does the plan fall down, or merely look less tidy on the slide.
